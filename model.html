The model

A product belongs to the people who carry it

BramLabs is a product studio. Products start here as experiments, and the ones that grow leave as their own companies. BramLabs holds 30 to 60 percent of each. The rest belongs to the people who take the product to success. This page is how that works.

01

Who ends up owning it

A product that grows is spun out of the studio into a company of its own. When that happens BramLabs holds between 30 and 60 percent of it. The other 40 to 70 percent belongs to the people who built it with us, love it, and take it to success.

Most products are built together and land somewhere in between. The studio does not take a majority by default. Where its share lands is argued from what the studio actually put in and what it risked, in the open, before spinout.

  • a
    OriginationWhose idea it was, and who saw that it was worth a product.
  • b
    Hands-on workHow much of the product the studio designed, built, and ran.
  • c
    CapitalWhat the studio paid for while the product could not pay for itself.
  • d
    TechnologyThe IP, components, and infrastructure the product stands on.
  • e
    Go-to-marketThe first users, the first customers, and the support that kept them.
  • f
    RiskWho stood to lose what if it had not worked.

02

Five phases, from idea to company

Every product moves through the same five phases. Ownership is on the table in each of them, so nobody hears a number for the first time at spinout.

  1. 01

    Idea

    The idea comes from inside the studio or from someone around it. People contribute loosely, and most ideas stop here.

    OwnershipNothing is allocated, and nothing is promised.
  2. 02

    Build

    We decide the idea is worth building, and a concrete team forms around it.

    OwnershipAn equity pool is set aside for this one product. Meaningful contributions are tracked in Product Units from here on.
  3. 03

    Launch

    The product ships to real users. Who does what, and who carries what, gets clearer.

    OwnershipThe team agrees a provisional split of the pool.
  4. 04

    Traction

    Paying customers, growing usage, retention that holds, and one person who is clearly the Product Lead.

    OwnershipThe split is largely settled, and formal vesting begins.
  5. 05

    Spinout

    The product has the traction, the lead, and the need for investment or the prospect of a sale to stand alone. It becomes its own company, and the product, its IP, and its business move into it.

    OwnershipBramLabs receives its stake. Earned units convert into shares or warrants in the new company.

03

How a stake is earned

Before a product is a company there are no shares in it to hand out, so we count in Product Units. Units measure each person’s part in one product, and they belong to that product only. Nobody earns a share of BramLabs by working on a product. They earn a share of the thing they are building.

Units are not fixed. They are adjusted at every review, on what a person actually contributed, the value it created, the responsibility they carry, the risk they take, and how far they commit to the product’s future.

Contribution creates eligibility. Responsibility creates ownership.

Hours do not convert into percentages. Someone who joins late and takes long-term responsibility for the product can earn more than an early contributor who no longer carries it. Someone who sells the product is normally paid a commission. Someone who takes on building and running it for years earns a real stake.

Units are not shares. Around traction they are translated into an approximate ownership split, and vesting starts. At spinout they convert into shares or warrants through an instrument that fits Danish tax and company law and each person’s role at that time. That step is worked through with a tax adviser before any share is issued or any IP is moved.

04

The Product Review

At every phase change the product gets a short review. It asks three questions.

  • a
    Does it go on?Continue, change direction, pause, or shut down.
  • b
    Who is the core team now?The people carrying the product today, which is not always the people who started it.
  • c
    Should the units change?Ownership follows what has happened since the last review, and what each person takes on next.

BramLabs sets the size of the pool for each product. The product team proposes how it is split. A small Product Council that knows the product approves the changes. The rules are the same for every product: who is eligible, how a stake vests, what happens when someone leaves, and what happens if the product is sold or shut down before spinout.

A review can end a product, and many should end. A studio that only knows how to keep going is building the wrong things slowly.

05

What the studio puts in

BramLabs earns its share with work. The studio is explicit about where it is strong, and it does not pretend to be world-class at every function.

  • a
    What we ownProduct engineering and AI-assisted development. Product shaping, UX, and visual identity. Direction and taste. And the launch stack: payments, auth, hosting, deployment, analytics, email, and support, already built, so no product starts by reinventing them.
  • b
    What we supportUser research, pricing, analytics, customer success, and the first steps of go-to-market. We coordinate these and do part of them ourselves.
  • c
    What we bring inSpecialist sales, growth marketing, legal and tax, fundraising, and deep domain expertise. These come from a network of people who know the model and join a product at the phase where it needs them.

We know how to build products fast, and what not to build.

Partners may take part in the upside of a specific product rather than only being paid as vendors. How is agreed product by product.

06

What the model is not

Equity in BramLabs. Ownership is earned in one product. The studio, its platform, and the way it builds stay with BramLabs.

Shares for hours. No one is owed a percentage for time spent. A stake follows value created, responsibility taken, risk, and long-term commitment.

A studio majority by default. BramLabs’ share is argued from what it contributed, product by product, inside the same range every time.

A surprise at the end. Ownership is discussed from the day a team forms and revisited at every review. By spinout the numbers are old news.

An offer. This page describes how we work. The terms that bind are the written ones for each product, checked by lawyers and tax advisers. We revise the page when the products teach us something.
