The model

A product belongs to the people who carry it

BramLabs is a product studio. Products start here as experiments, and the ones that grow leave as their own companies. BramLabs keeps a share of each. The rest belongs to the people who take the product to success.

01

Who owns it

A product that grows is spun out of the studio into a company of its own. BramLabs holds X percent of that company. The rest belongs to the people who built the product with us, love it, and take it to success.

02

How a stake is earned

Nobody earns a share of BramLabs by working on a product. They earn a share of the thing they are building, and only that.

Contribution creates eligibility. Responsibility creates ownership.

A stake is not paid out by the hour. It follows the value a person creates, the responsibility they take on, and how far they commit to the product’s future. Someone who joins late and carries the product can earn more than someone who was early and moved on.

We talk about ownership from the day a team forms and come back to it as the product grows, so it is settled long before the product becomes a company.

03

From idea to company

Every product moves through the same five phases. At each step we ask whether it should go on, and who is carrying it now.

  • 01
    IdeaSomeone sees a problem that is worth a product.
  • 02
    BuildA small team forms and builds it with the studio.
  • 03
    LaunchIt goes to real users, early.
  • 04
    TractionUsers and customers show that it deserves to grow.
  • 05
    SpinoutIt becomes its own company, owned by the studio and the people who carry it.

Most experiments stop along the way. That is part of the model, not a failure of it.

04

What the studio puts in

BramLabs earns its share with work: engineering with AI, product shaping, design, taste, and a launch stack that every product reuses instead of reinventing. Where we are not the strongest, we bring in partners who are.

We know how to build products fast, and what not to build.

This page is an outline of how we think. The actual terms are agreed product by product.
